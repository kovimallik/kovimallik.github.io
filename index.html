Research interests

From roots to field trials

I work with breeders to connect variation in the genome to the traits we can measure in a crop.

Perennial ryegrass

Which grasses make better use of nitrogen?

We grow 40 cultivars at three fertiliser levels, measuring yield alongside drone and robot observations. In the lab, we measure nitrogen uptake in contrasting plants and use single-nucleus sequencing to find which root cells express nitrate transporters.

Our aim is to identify grasses that produce more forage at a given nitrogen input, or hold their yield when that input falls.

Explore the ryegrass root atlas

Timothy · genomic selection

Can a model predict the next breeding cycle?

With Graminor, we compared nine models in 889 full-sib timothy families and tested them in 213 independent families. Prediction accuracy fell when models met new families.

Testing across breeding cycles shows breeders how much confidence to place in a genomic ranking before the next field trial.

Read the study in Theoretical and Applied Genetics

Timothy · perennial ryegrass

What changes when grass seed moves south?

Seed of northern cultivars can be multiplied at lower latitudes. We track allele frequencies, winter survival and gene expression across generations to understand how multiplication environments influence adaptation.

Read the timothy population genetics study

Red clover · meadow fescue · potato

How can genetics help breeders choose their material?

In tetraploid red clover, we compared gene expression in plants with contrasting seed set. In meadow fescue and potato, my work makes genome information easier for breeders to use when they evaluate traits and candidate genes.

Research funding

The project portfolio

These projects connect questions about how crops use nitrogen with the decisions breeders and farmers make in the field.

Funded projectBudget · NOK
Soil2Milk2026–2030 · Project leader17.3m

How do choices in a grass field affect milk production? We follow nitrogen through ryegrass and legume mixtures, forage quality and dairy cows, measuring nitrogen losses and methane emissions along the way.

NOK 16m from the Research Council of Norway and NOK 1.3m from industry. Partners include TINE, Graminor, Yara and Norwegian Agricultural Extension Service.

Soil2Milk at NMBU
DLT-Farming2023–2027 · Project leader12m

We compare 40 perennial ryegrass cultivars at Ås and Hamar under three nitrogen regimes. Field robots, drones and ground sensors help us relate canopy measurements to dry matter yield, forage quality and genetic variation.

NOK 9.86m in public funding and NOK 2.14m from industry. Partners include Graminor, BioDrone and Accenture.

DLT-Farming at NMBU
TWIN-NUE2026–2029 · Project leader5.5m

Daily 3D scans reveal how perennial ryegrass and oat grow under different nitrogen supplies. We combine these measurements with genomic data to model growth and predict how individual genotypes respond to fertiliser.

Funded by NMBU and the Norwegian Plant Phenotyping Infrastructure (PheNo).

TWIN-NUE at NMBU
Ryegrass nitrogen genetics2026–2029 · Doctoral project lead5m

This NMBU-funded doctoral project uses gene editing to test how candidate genes influence nitrogen use in perennial ryegrass. Molecular studies of nitrogen uptake help identify plants that make better use of the nitrogen available.

Doctoral research at SustainPlant
GE-Sustain2024–2027 · Work package leader3m

I lead gene discovery for potato disease resistance and processing quality. The team studies genetic targets to help breeders improve resistance to late blight and select for tuber quality.

NOK 3m covers my work package within the NOK 16m project led by Graminor.

GE-Sustain at SustainPlant
ProteinSenseFrom 2026 · Project leader0.75m

Grass protein changes as the crop matures. With TINE, Yara and Graminor, we are testing whether drone images and field measurements can forecast crude protein and help farmers choose a harvest date.

Funded by FFL/JA.

With Graminor, we screened perennial ryegrass cultivars in hydroponics and developed gene-editing targets for nitrogen transport and metabolism. This FFL/JA pilot established a basis for testing gene function in contrasting cultivars.
