Research interests

I use field trials, genomics and root transcriptomics to study yield and winter survival in timothy and perennial ryegrass. I also investigate late blight resistance in potato and how cereal varieties respond to nitrogen fertiliser.

Nitrogen use in perennial ryegrass

In Ås and Hamar, we compare forage yield at three nitrogen levels. We then grow contrasting ryegrasses in hydroponics and use single-nucleus sequencing to identify the root cells that express nitrate transporters.

We want to know which cultivars yield more at the same fertiliser rate, and which retain their yield when the rate falls.

Browse the ryegrass root atlas

Potato disease resistance and nitrogen use in cereals

In GE-Sustain, I lead the work to identify potato genes associated with late blight resistance and tuber quality for processing. One trait we study is the amount of asparagine in the tuber.

In TWIN-NUE, we use repeated 3D scans to compare the growth of oat and perennial ryegrass at different nitrogen levels. I am extending this work to barley to find out which varieties yield more at the same nitrogen rate or maintain yield when the rate is reduced.

Testing genomic predictions in new families

With Graminor, we compared nine genomic prediction models in timothy and tested them in 213 independent families. Mean predictive correlation fell from 0.62 in cross-validation to 0.16 in the independent families.

For a breeder choosing parents, the second test is closer to the decision that matters.

Read the timothy study

Winter survival across seed generations

Seed of northern timothy cultivars is often multiplied farther south. We follow allele frequencies and gene expression across seed generations and test how plants recover from freezing and ice encasement.

The breeding question is whether a cultivar retains the winter hardiness it was selected for.

Read the winter stress study

Seed yield and genetic resources

In tetraploid red clover, we compared gene expression in plants with contrasting seed set. In meadow fescue, we developed a public genome database to help researchers locate genes and compare sequences.

Soil2Milk2026–2030 · Project leader 17.3 million NOK

We trace nitrogen from grass and clover fields through forage and dairy cows, measuring feed quality, nitrogen losses and methane emissions. The Research Council of Norway contributes NOK 16 million and industry partners contribute NOK 1.3 million. Partners include TINE, Graminor, Yara and the Norwegian Agricultural Extension Service.

Soil2Milk at NMBU
DLT-Farming2023–2027 · Project leader 12 million NOK

At Ås and Hamar, we compare 40 perennial ryegrass cultivars under three nitrogen regimes. Field robots, drones and sensors help us relate canopy measurements to dry matter yield and forage quality. The budget includes NOK 9.86 million in public funding and NOK 2.14 million from industry. Partners include Graminor, BioDrone and Accenture.

DLT-Farming at NMBU
TWIN-NUE2026–2029 · Project leader 5.5 million NOK

Daily 3D scans record how ryegrass and oat grow at different nitrogen levels. We combine those measurements with genomic data to model how individual genotypes respond to fertiliser. NMBU and the Norwegian Plant Phenotyping Infrastructure (PheNo) fund the project.

Teaching and supervision

In the field, students compare forage crops on the ground. In the genomics classroom, they analyse data from our trials and defend their interpretation.

Courses at NMBU

  • PJH212

    Forage and Seed Crop Production

    Course coordinator and lecturer, since 2026

    I coordinate the teaching and assessment, including field classes at Vollebekk. Students revise their reports after feedback and present their findings to the class.

  • BIN300

    Statistical Genomics

    Sequencing module lead, since 2015

    I teach RNA sequencing and variant analysis. In the practical, students work through a dataset from our research and explain what their results mean for the plant.

  • BIO321 / BIO421

    Population Genetics

    Co-teacher, 2024–2025

    I taught about half of this MSc and PhD course, using breeding populations to explain genetic drift, selection and effective population size.

Guest teaching and pedagogical training

My guest lectures at NMBU cover plant biotechnology (BIO244), gene function (BIO327) and phenotyping (BIO312). I also taught transcriptome analysis to PhD candidates at TalTech in Estonia and LAMMC in Lithuania through EditGrass4Food.

I completed NMBU’s course in master’s and doctoral supervision in 2023 (5 ECTS). In 2026–2027 I am studying university pedagogy at Østfold University College (10 ECTS).
